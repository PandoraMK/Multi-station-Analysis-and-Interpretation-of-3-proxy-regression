Multi-Proxy Ionospheric Model Specification

This research evaluates long-term Total Electron Content (TEC) variations over Southern Africa using GNSS data from the Sutherland (SUTH) station from 2000 to 2024.

The multi-proxy ordinary least squares (OLS) regression model is specified as:

VTEC = β₀ + β₁·t + β₂·F10.7 + β₃·MgII + β₄·SNN + ϵ

Data Preprocessing Workflow

1. Temporal Filtering

Restricted to local daytime hours (10:00 – 14:00 UTC) to capture peak daily photoionization.

2. Seasonal Decomposition

Cubic interpolation of 18 missing monthly points (6.25%) followed by additive decomposition (period = 12 months) to extract deseasonalized VTEC.

3. Z-Score Standardization

Solar proxy variables (F10.7, MgII, Sunspot Number) independently standardized to ensure numerically stable OLS estimation.

4. Back-Transformation

Standardized coefficients converted back to physical units for direct geophysical interpretation.

Key Takeaways

  • High Explanatory Power: The full model achieves an $R^2$ of 0.9107, successfully capturing solar cycle variations.
  • MgII Dominance: MgII serves as the primary statistically significant solar proxy ($p < 0.001$), explaining ~25.5% partial variance.
  • Collinearity Notice: F10.7 and Sunspot Number exhibit high collinearity with MgII, resulting in inflated standard errors in the full OLS model.

Geophysical Interpretation

The low Durbin-Watson statistic ($0.776$) indicates that consecutive monthly residuals share unmodeled persistence. This is typical in ionospheric studies where thermospheric composition changes and plasma transport have memory beyond monthly aggregation.

Future Enhancements: Incorporating Cochrane-Orcutt AR(1) error correction or ARIMAX structures will eliminate residual autocorrelation and provide robust standard errors.
